# Architecture review: park-pending-inbound

Dimension: **architecture** only. Judged against the specflow block's "How to
shape a change" in `CLAUDE.md`, the transport-versus-application rule ("SDS is
transport"), the "keep decisions out of the adapter" trap, and the design's own
claims. Reviewed at `30b9d14f`; `cargo test -p dialectica-core --lib delivery`
is green (138 tests) before any mutation of mine.

Genuine defects are marked **defect**. Everything marked **preference** is a
shape argument the owner may decline.

- [ ] **`dev-writer`** — `delivery.rs:861-876`, `delivery.rs:887`, `delivery.rs:1526-1540` — **defect (low)** — the "read once" claim is true for the park-or-judge choice and false for the Stoa the judge then uses
      `Channels::take` reads the channel's state once, under the lock (`on_take`), and returns `Taken::Judge` with no Stoa in it. `Processor::pass` then resolves the channel to a Stoa through `Channels::stoa_of`, a second lock acquisition after the first was released. The spec says "The state is read once for each payload, and the payload is parked or judged on that one reading", and design Decision 1 repeats it. The two readings can disagree.
      **Scenario:** a payload is taken on a channel that is neither open nor being opened (its open was declined after the payload was handed over). `on_take` returns `Judge`, meaning "refuse as an unknown channel". Before `pass` calls `stoa_of`, the worker settles a fresh join of the same Stoa as held. `stoa_of` now finds the Stoa and the payload is stored instead of refused. Not reproduced: the window is the gap between two lock acquisitions on one thread, so no deterministic test reaches it. It is reasoned from the code, and harmless in effect (an op is admitted that the spec says to refuse), but the guarantee then holds by timing and not by construction.
      **Shape that removes it:** make the lookup part of the one reading, `Taken::Judge(Option<Address>)` filled in `Channels::take` under the lock, and give `transport::receive_via` the resolved value for the take path. A review's `Held` path keeps asking at judge time, because the spec says reviews are judged "against the channels open when it is judged". That keeps the two timings as two named code paths and not an accident of one function.

- [ ] **`dev-writer`** — `parked.rs:441-457`, `parked.rs:482`, `delivery.rs:1204-1215` — **preference (low-medium)** — the "one shedding rule" is one comparison function and two hand-built inputs
      Design Decision 5 says the queue and the parked messages share "one rule and one function". What is shared is `shed`, the `max_by`. The part that makes the spec's tie-break come out right is **counting the arrival with its own channel as the newest of all** (`own.newest = u64::MAX`), and that is written out twice: in `parked::channel_loads` and inline in `InboundQueue::offer`. Each caller also finds and removes the victim's newest message on its own (`max_by_key` over rows; `rposition` over the deque). The next bound added to either side has to remember the `u64::MAX` step; that is the "fourth slightly-different copy" signal at two copies.
      Separately, `shed` and `Load` live in `parked.rs`, the persistence module, so `delivery`'s in-memory queue imports its policy from a store. Nothing in `parked.rs` needs it except `plan_park`.
      **Measured:** this is not a coverage gap. Deleting `own.newest = u64::MAX` from `offer` fails `the_arriving_payloads_channel_loses_a_tie_for_the_most_waiting`; deleting it from `channel_loads` fails `parked::tests::the_arriving_payloads_channel_loses_a_tie_for_the_most_parked`. Each copy is pinned by its own test, which is the cost of having two.
      **Shape:** one function that takes the held loads and the arrival (`fn shed_for(held: impl Iterator<Item = (&str, measure, place)>, arriving: (&str, measure)) -> Option<&str>`), so the arrival's "newest of all" is written once, and in a place neither the queue nor the store owns.

- [ ] **`dev-writer`** — `design.md` Risks; `parked.rs:248-275`, `parked.rs:348-362`, `delivery.rs:1432-1450` — **defect (low), a cost nobody measured** — every park is a synchronous, fsynced, whole-table transaction on the one thread that serves every Stoa
      The change exists because one processor serving every Stoa stalled behind one channel's open. Parking moves the stall from "an open's answer" to "a disk write". `Processor::park` opens `parked.sqlite`, runs `BEGIN IMMEDIATE`, `held_rows` reads **every** parked row (up to 1024) into a `Vec<Row>` for the pure `plan_park`, and commits, per message. `Review::Held` likewise decides up to 256 messages on that thread, each opening the op log, before the processor takes another payload. Design Risks names the per-answer open of the file and the review's memory; it does not name this.
      **Measured:** a throwaway test (not committed) parked 1024 messages of 600 bytes across 4 channels, opening the store per park as `Processor::park` does: **1.26 s** with the file on tmpfs and **4.67 s** with it on this machine's btrfs-on-LUKS disk, about 4.6 ms per park. A 256-message burst on one channel, at that per-park cost, would hold the processor about 1.2 s (extrapolated, not run), during which the 256-slot queue fills for every other Stoa. Bounded and survivable, so low; but the design's whole argument is about head-of-line blocking, and this is the remaining one.
      **What to do:** record the measurement and the head-of-line cost in design.md's Risks. If it is worth cutting, `plan_park` needs per-channel counts and byte sums (a `GROUP BY channel` and the newest `seq` per channel), not every row; and a burst could keep one connection open across consecutive parks. Either is optional; the missing line in Risks is not.

- [ ] **`dev-writer`** — `parked.rs:113-239` — **preference (low), deferrable** — a fifth hand-copied SQLite store skeleton
      `parked.rs` re-implements, again, what `log/sqlite.rs`, `sender.rs`, `membership.rs` and `identity_store.rs` each already carry: an error enum with `Storage`, `UnknownLayoutVersion` and `LayoutDoesNotMatchItsVersion` plus its `Display`; `in_memory`; `from_connection`; a `check_layout` that names the columns; a `create_schema` with the `user_version` commit point; and a `storage()` mapper. `grep -rn "fn create_schema" dialectica-core/src` returns five hits, and the copies have already drifted: `sender.rs` uses a plain `BEGIN` with the version read outside the transaction and a comment saying it is safe only because one thread opens it; `parked.rs` and the op log use `BEGIN IMMEDIATE` and read the version under the lock. CLAUDE.md names this: "the fourth slightly-different copy of a guard is the signal to reshape". This change did not create the shape, but it is the fifth copy, and it copied the `BEGIN IMMEDIATE` reasoning into a file that only one thread opens.
      **Shape:** a small shared helper (open, read-or-create the layout stamp under `BEGIN IMMEDIATE`, run a store's `check_layout` closure) each store calls with its own table SQL and version, in its own no-behaviour commit before or after this piece. Reasonable to defer to its own issue; say where it went.

- [ ] **`dev-writer`** — `delivery/tests.rs:584-615` — **preference (low)** — a structural property is pinned by counting substrings in the source text
      `the_running_wiring_builds_its_processor_with_the_one_constructor_and_never_changes_its_bounds` strips comments and whitespace from `include_str!("../delivery.rs")` and asserts `bounds:` occurs exactly twice, `Processor::new(` exactly once, `bounds=` never. It exists because `Processor::bounds` is a field that tests shrink, so "start does not change it" cannot be seen by running anything. CLAUDE.md says to put the complexity in the data structure: make the invariant hold by construction (`Processor::new` takes no bounds and sets `PARK_BOUNDS`; a `#[cfg(test)] fn with_bounds(self, ParkBounds) -> Self` is the only way to differ, so no production path can reach it), and the text test is unnecessary. As written it also fails for an unrelated reason the day any struct in that 1800-line file gains a field called `bounds`.
      The same file keeps a cfg(test)-only flag in the production `Shared` (`closed`, read in `Channels::take`); the same remedy applies to it but it is not worth a box of its own.

- [ ] **`dev-writer`** — `design.md` Decision 5, `delivery.rs:1174-1179` — **preference (low)** — the "newest goes first" rule leans on transport arrival order, and says so as a fact
      CLAUDE.md's layering rule: ordering and recency at forum scope are the application's, carried in the signed op, and a design that depends on what the transport hands it breaks when the transport changes. The shedding rule has no choice here (a parked or waiting payload is unverified and undecoded, so its op counter cannot be read) and "newest" can only mean "handed over last". That is a fair reason to use it. The claim attached to it, "a backlog arrives roughly oldest first, so the newest messages are leaves of their threads", is an observation about SDS's catch-up order that nothing in the repository measures, and the rule now also governs what is kept on disk. Say in Decision 5 that this is a loss-minimising heuristic on unmeasured transport behaviour and that no correctness property depends on it (a discarded op is a missing op until another peer's copy arrives, and ordering stays the Lamport clock's); do not leave it reading as established.

- [ ] **`dev-writer`** — `delivery.rs` (whole file, 1824 lines) — **preference (low)** — the change grew the module that already holds many jobs instead of making room first
      `delivery.rs` holds, among other things, the delivery seam and reply parsing, the node config, the journal and a 29-variant `Note`, the pure channel state machine (`ChannelBook`, `Taken`, `Review`), the lock that orders it (`Channels`), the bounded queue, the worker, the listener, the processor and the lifecycle. The parked store went into its own file, which is right, and the tests into `tests/parking.rs`, also right. But `ChannelBook`, `Taken`, `Review` and `InboundQueue` are pure data and rules with no I/O and no threads, and they are where this change's decisions live (`on_take`, `settle`, `startup_review`, `offer`); they would sit well in `delivery/book.rs`, next to the tests that drive them. CLAUDE.md: "make the change easy, then make the easy change" — a no-behaviour move first, in its own commit. A follow-up is fine; this is not a merge blocker.

## Areas that were clean

- **The adapter, `dialectica/rust-lib/src/lib.rs`, is untouched by this change** (`git diff ad5c7e43 HEAD --stat` lists no change to it) and still holds only the four one-line `Delivery` calls, the event subscription, `Stores::in_dir(...)` and `Delivering::start`. No decision about parking, bounds, reviews or the clock entered it. It cannot be built by `cargo test`; I did not run `nix build ./dialectica#lgx`, and since the file is unchanged there is nothing new for that gate to see.
- **Transport versus application.** What the transport tells us (sender identifier, event timestamp) is neither stored nor consulted: `parked.sqlite`'s table has no column for either, `parked_inbound` hands the boundary `""` and `0`, and the window is read from this peer's clock at the review. Nothing signed or authoritative is derived from arrival order; `seq` is a local sequence used for review order and shedding only. The parking is built above the transport, in core, not worked around inside it, which is what CLAUDE.md asks.
- **A store kept apart from the op log.** `parked.sqlite` is its own file with its own layout stamp, `Stores::parked` is called only from the processor, nothing else in the crate or the adapter opens it, no store enumerates or wipes the directory, and the op log is never handed a parked row except through `transport::admit` at a review. "Nothing unverified may be readable as an op" holds by file boundary, as design Decision 2 says, and not by a filter.
- **The single lock.** `book`, `waiting` and `reviews` share `Channels::shared`; `settle` changes the book and queues its review in one critical section, and `take` returns any review before any payload. I swapped the order in `take` (payload first): `a_review_is_taken_before_a_payload_that_was_waiting_when_its_event_came` and `parked_messages_are_decided_before_later_messages_on_their_channel_in_hand_over_order` both fail. The design's reasons for rejecting a marker in the payload queue, a worker that runs reviews, and two locks hold up against the code. The lock is held only for map and queue work, plus the encode in `Channels::handoff` that the module doc already declares. The one-processor-thread assumption that the ordering rests on is enforced by `Delivering::start`'s `Wiring` state and by `Processor::run(self)` consuming the processor, and is documented.
- **Review and settle triggers.** `Review` is constructed in exactly two places, `ChannelBook::settle` and `ChannelBook::startup_review`; `Opening`'s `Drop` guard settles every path that never reaches delivery, and `Opening::settle` before the log line (Decision 9) is what makes "nothing is parked afterwards" independent of timing. A closed worker, a refused send, and a worker that never started all end in `Review::Unopened`.
- **`parked.rs` itself.** No `unwrap`, `expect` or indexing outside tests; `take_channel` selects and deletes in one `BEGIN IMMEDIATE` transaction; `plan_park` is a pure function of the held rows and is applied by the store in one transaction. The bound ordering is held at compile time and again against literals.
